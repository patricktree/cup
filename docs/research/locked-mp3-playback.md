# Locked-screen playback of one long MP3

> Historical research, incorporated 2026-10-27 by [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-serve-one-narration-track-and-play-it-with-one-audio-element.md). Cup adopted one continuous `<audio>` element with `preload="metadata"`, Media Session metadata without action handlers, and a [signed media URL](../architecture/authentication.md#signed-media-url) that stays valid for a whole listen, renewed on an in-app Play. Its "Cup today" section describes the element pair and media cookie that this change removed. The device behavior it could not observe was checked by the operator in [issue #72](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/72). External claims have not been reverified.

[Original investigation](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/8fc211666488d7c42b7480318365f92ed3b64502/docs/research/locked-mp3-playback.md).

Research date: 2026-10-27. Question: [issue #58](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/58), part of the [narration track map](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/56).

How do iOS Safari and Android Chrome fetch and keep playing one long MP3 (CBR, 30–90 minutes, served with byte ranges and a cookie) from a single `<audio>` element while the device is locked, and do their default media controls suffice?

## Sources and limits

Chromium and WebKit claims come from their source on `main`, read on 2026-10-27 at about Chromium [`c160f0f0`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b) and WebKit [`d1cdb930`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/commit/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89). Links below point at those revisions; the functions are named because line numbers drift. Shipping browsers lag `main` by weeks to months, so a device can still behave differently.

Limits:

- iOS Safari's media loading is driven by AVFoundation, which is closed source. WebKit's source shows how AVFoundation's requests reach the network, but not when AVFoundation issues them or how far it reads ahead. That part is **unknown** and needs the device experiment in [Experiments that would settle the unknowns](#experiments-that-would-settle-the-unknowns).
- Reading source establishes what the code does; whether a given Chrome or iOS release ships that code, and how Android or iOS process management interferes, is only settled on a device. No physical device was used.
- Android System WebView (the Capacitor shell) and home-screen web apps on iOS were not researched; [WebKit bug 261858](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261858) shows standalone web apps on iOS can behave worse than Safari.

## Cup today

- The web player creates elements with `new Audio()`, `preload = "auto"` and `crossOrigin = "use-credentials"` ([`create-playback.ts`](../../apps/web-app/src/app/player/create-playback.ts)); the API is same-origin with the web app.
- The media cookie `cup_media` holds the bearer JWT itself, scoped `Path=/api/files; HttpOnly; SameSite=Strict`, with `Max-Age` equal to the token's remaining lifetime ([`account-auth.ts`](../../libs/api-server/src/http/account-auth.ts) `mediaCookie`, issued by `filesSession` in [`api-server.ts`](../../libs/api-server/src/api-server.ts)). Even a cookie a browser kept would fail verification once the JWT's `exp` passes. A missing or expired credential gets `401 account-session-required` ([`http-errors.ts`](../../libs/api-server/src/http/http-errors.ts)).
- Delivery answers `Range: bytes=N-`, `bytes=N-M` and suffix ranges with `206` and `Content-Range`, a plain request with `200`, and sends `Accept-Ranges: bytes` and an `ETag` ([`serve-audiobook.ts`](../../libs/api-server/src/http/serve-audiobook.ts)). Responses default to `Cache-Control: private, no-store`.
- The client requests a fresh cookie on load, on every Play and before each preload ([private media access](../architecture/authentication.md#private-media-access)). A Play pressed on the lock screen without a page action handler never reaches that code.
- [`registerPlayerMediaSession`](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/0fb4e71d/apps/web-app/src/platform/player.ts) sets the title and handlers for play, pause, next track, previous track and seek-to, and publishes position state.

## Findings

### 1. When each browser issues byte-range requests

**Android Chrome (confirmed from source).** Chrome reads one open-ended request at a time and pauses reading instead of finishing it:

- Each load is a single request with `Range: bytes=<position>-` (right-unbounded), sent with the element's credentials mode ([`ResourceMultiBufferDataProvider::Start`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/resource_multi_buffer_data_provider.cc)).
- Read-ahead is a byte window with hysteresis ([`MultiBufferDataSource::UpdateBufferSizes`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/multi_buffer_data_source.cc)). It targets 10 seconds of the bitrate (`preload_seconds_`, [header](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/multi_buffer_data_source.h)), clamped to at least 2 MiB (`kMinBufferPreload`), plus a bonus of 10% of the bytes read so far capped at that same amount, plus 1 MiB (`kPreloadHighExtra`) of hysteresis. Loading continues until the high mark is buffered ahead, then stops until the buffer falls below the low mark ([`MultiBufferReader`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/multi_buffer_reader.cc)).
- For a 128 kbps track (16,000 bytes per second) 10 seconds is far below 2 MiB, so the minimum governs: at the start Chrome buffers 3 MiB (about 3 minutes 17 seconds) and tops up whenever less than 2 MiB (about 2 minutes 11 seconds) remains. After about 20 MiB (about 22 minutes) has been read, the bonus is maxed out and the marks become 5 MiB (about 5 minutes 28 seconds) and 4 MiB (about 4 minutes 22 seconds). Chrome therefore never buffers a 30–90 minute track early (57.6 MB per hour at 128 kbps); it stays at most about 5½ minutes ahead for the whole session, locked or not. Files under 25 MiB (`kDefaultPinSize`) are kept in the cache whole once read, which does not apply here.
- Pausing the read does not end the request: `SetDeferred` calls `SetDefersLoading` on the same loader, so data resumes on the same response. A new request, and with it a new cookie check, is issued only when that response ends early or fails (retried from the current position, see [§2](#2-a-failed-range-request-mid-playback)), after a seek outside the buffer, or after the player was suspended.
- A paused player is marked stale after 15 seconds idle ([`RendererWebMediaPlayerDelegate`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/content/renderer/media/renderer_web_media_player_delegate.cc), `idle_timeout_`) and then idle-suspended ([`WebMediaPlayerImpl::UpdatePlayState_ComputePlayState`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/web_media_player_impl.cc)), so resuming after a pause longer than that can start a new request from the current position.

**iOS Safari (partly confirmed).** WebKit hands the URL to AVFoundation but routes every request AVFoundation makes through its own loader:

- `MediaPlayerPrivateAVFoundationObjC::createAVAssetForURL` sets `AVURLAssetUseClientURLLoadingExclusively` (or `AVURLAssetRequiresCustomURLLoadingKey`) and installs a `WebCoreNSURLSession` as the asset's URL session ([source](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/avfoundation/objc/MediaPlayerPrivateAVFoundationObjC.mm)). Each AVFoundation data task becomes a WebKit `MediaResourceLoader` request with `Credentials::Include` and caching disallowed ([`MediaResourceLoader::requestResource`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/loader/MediaResourceLoader.cpp), [`WebCoreNSURLSession.mm`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/platform/network/cocoa/WebCoreNSURLSession.mm)). So **each range request carries whatever cookie the page's cookie store holds at that moment**, and an expired cookie means a request without it. (WebKit also snapshots cookies into `AVURLAssetHTTPCookiesKey` when the asset is created, which matters only where AVFoundation loads by itself.)
- WebKit does not set `preferredForwardBufferDuration` (no occurrence in the player source). Apple documents that with the default of 0 "the player will choose an appropriate level of buffering for most use cases" ([AVPlayerItem.preferredForwardBufferDuration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avplayeritem/preferredforwardbufferduration)). WebKit only steers AVFoundation's resource conservation: playing elements get the default level, while a paused element in a hidden page gets `MakeResourcesPurgeable` (`AVPlayerResourceConservationLevelReuseActivePlayerResources`) ([`MediaElementSession::preferredBufferingPolicy`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/html/MediaElementSession.cpp), `setBufferingPolicy` in the player source), so buffered data may be dropped during a locked pause and fetched again on resume.
- **Unknown:** the sizes of AVFoundation's ranges, how far ahead of the playhead it reads, whether that changes while locked or on cellular, and whether it ever reads a 30–90 minute file to the end early. Nothing primary documents this; the experiment below measures it.

**Consequence for media authorization (confirmed for Chrome, expected for iOS).** Neither browser downloads the track early. Both keep fetching throughout playback without any page JavaScript: Chrome whenever its single response is interrupted or playback resumes after an idle suspension; iOS whenever AVFoundation decides to. The credential must therefore be valid at any moment from Play to the end of the track and after any pause, not just when playback starts. With the cookie bound to a JWT of about one hour, a 30–90 minute track, or a shorter one paused and resumed later, can reach a request whose cookie has expired. Whether Chrome actually issues a second request during one uninterrupted listen depends on whether its deferred connection survives the network path and Cloudflare's edge, which is **unknown**.

### 2. A failed range request mid-playback

**Chrome (confirmed from source)**, in [`ResourceMultiBufferDataProvider`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/resource_multi_buffer_data_provider.cc):

- An HTTP answer other than `206` (verified), `200` or `416`, such as `401`, fails the resource at once (`DidReceiveResponse` sets `do_fail`); there is no retry. Playback continues from the buffer (at most about 5½ minutes) and then the element reports a network error.
- A transport failure (`DidFail`, e.g. network loss) is retried up to `kMaxRetries = 30` times ([header](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/resource_multi_buffer_data_provider.h)) with a delay growing from 300 ms by 50 ms per attempt, about 30 seconds in total. A response that ends before the known length is retried after 25 ms. Retries resume from the current position and need a valid cookie. Blocked requests, certificate errors and Service Worker responses are not retried.

**iOS (unknown).** A `401` reaches AVFoundation through `WebCoreNSURLSession`; how AVFoundation reacts (fail the item, stall, retry) is not visible in WebKit's source. Expect a stall and an `error` or `stalled` event once the buffer runs out.

**Resuming without page JavaScript (confirmed: it cannot).** After a fatal media error the element does not reload by itself; only script calling `load()` and seeking can recover. While locked that script may not run in time: on iOS the WebContent process loses its media assertion as soon as audio stops (see [§3](#3-page-javascript-while-locked)), and [WebKit bug 173332](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=173332) and [WebKit bug 261554](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261554) record years of iOS regressions where starting audio from the background produced no sound. The spec should therefore not rely on locked recovery and should make mid-session failures impossible by keeping the credential valid; recovery belongs to the next foreground Play.

### 3. Page JavaScript while locked

**Android Chrome (confirmed from source).** An audible page is exempt from freezing wherever the performance manager freezes pages (whether that policy runs on Android was not checked): Chromium's freezing policy records `kAudible` and, after audio stops, `kRecentlyAudible` as reasons a page cannot be frozen ([`freezing_policy.cc`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/components/performance_manager/freezing/freezing_policy.cc)). Chrome's timer throttling leaves timers unthrottled while the page has made sound in the last 30 seconds; a silent hidden page gets 1-second alignment, and after 5 minutes hidden and 30 seconds silent, one wake-up per minute ([Chrome 88 timer throttling](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88)). Media events keep firing. Chrome keeps its process alive with a `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK` foreground service while media plays ([`MediaNotificationController`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/components/browser_ui/media/android/java/src/org/chromium/components/browser_ui/media/MediaNotificationController.java)); while paused, Android may stop or kill the app.

**iOS Safari (confirmed from source).** The UI process holds a `MediaPlayback` process assertion for a WebContent process only while one of its pages is playing audio, and releases it as soon as none is ([`WebProcessProxy::updateAudibleMediaAssertions`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebKit/UIProcess/WebProcessProxy.cpp)). While audible, timers in the hidden page are aligned to 1 second ([`Page::updateTimerThrottlingState`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/page/Page.cpp), [`DOMTimer::hiddenPageAlignmentInterval`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/page/DOMTimer.h)). When audio stops (pause, `ended`, error, buffering stall that stops output), the process can be suspended, and then no script, timer or event handler runs until it resumes ([bug 173332](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=173332): the `ended` event is delivered only after the process resumes).

**Rule for the spec:** nothing that playback correctness depends on may need page JavaScript while locked. Event handlers and 1-second timers do run during audible playback on both platforms, so best-effort work there (saving the listening position, highlighting) is fine; anything at a pause, an end or an error is not guaranteed to run until the page is in the foreground again.

### 4. Lock-screen and notification controls without action handlers

**iOS Safari (confirmed from source).** With no action handler registered, WebKit registers its default command set with MediaRemote: play, pause, toggle play/pause, begin/end seeking forward and backward, seek to position, and skip forward and backward with a 15-second interval; seek commands are dropped when the media is not seekable ([`RemoteCommandListenerCocoa::updateSupportedCommands`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/platform/cocoa/RemoteCommandListenerCocoa.mm), `defaultCommands`). The element handles them itself, skipping 15 seconds by default ([`HTMLMediaElement::didReceiveRemoteControlCommand`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/html/HTMLMediaElement.cpp)), and commands go to page handlers only when the page has active ones ([`MediaElementSession::didReceiveRemoteControlCommand`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/html/MediaElementSession.cpp)). Registering any handler replaces the defaults with the registered commands plus play and pause, which is why Cup's current next/previous handlers show track buttons instead of ±15 s. Duration and elapsed time come from the element ([`MediaSessionManagerCocoa::setNowPlayingInfo`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/cocoa/MediaSessionManagerCocoa.mm)), so the lock screen shows a working scrubber, ±15 s buttons and play/pause by default.

**Android Chrome (confirmed from source, one point to verify).** For a controllable session Chrome adds play, pause, stop, seek-to, scrub-to, seek forward and seek backward to the available actions even without handlers, and uses the single player's own position when the page set no position state ([`MediaSessionImpl::RebuildAndNotifyActionsChanged`, `RebuildAndNotifyMediaPositionChanged`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/content/browser/media/session/media_session_impl.cc)). The notification maps these to Android media session actions, including `ACTION_SEEK_TO`, and publishes the duration, so the system media controls show a seek bar; a seek-to falls back to seeking the player when no handler exists (`MediaSessionImpl::SeekTo`). The seek forward/backward buttons appear by default, but pressing one is forwarded to the page as an action ([`MediaSessionHelper`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/components/browser_ui/media/android/java/src/org/chromium/components/browser_ui/media/MediaSessionHelper.java) → `MediaSessionImpl::DidReceiveAction`), and Blink ignores an action without a handler ([`MediaSession::DidReceiveAction`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/mediasession/media_session.cc)). By this reading, those two buttons are visible but do nothing unless the page registers `seekforward` and `seekbackward` handlers; **verify on a device**.

**What metadata adds (confirmed).** Setting only `navigator.mediaSession.metadata` replaces the title (otherwise the page title) and supplies artwork (Chrome otherwise falls back to the site's favicon, per `MediaSessionHelper`); it registers no command and does not change the defaults above. The [Media Session spec](https://w3c.github.io/mediasession/) (Editor's Draft, 5 June 2026) recommends default play and pause handling and deriving position state from media elements, which both engines do.

**Do the defaults suffice?** On iOS, yes: play/pause, a scrubber and ±15 s. On Android: play/pause and a seek bar work; the seek forward/backward buttons likely need `seekforward` and `seekbackward` handlers, which can be one-liners on the element (`element.currentTime ± offset`) without reintroducing player state. A handler changes iOS too (its skip buttons then come from the page). A `play` handler is the only way to run Cup's code, for example a credential refresh, before a lock-screen Play resumes the element; default Play resumes without any page code.

### 5. Causes of a single element pausing shortly after locking

Confirmed causes in the source, each avoidable:

- **A `<video>` element, or media with a video track, on iOS.** iOS restricts `Video` and `VideoAudio` sessions with `BackgroundProcessPlaybackRestricted` and `SuspendedUnderLockPlaybackRestricted`, and interrupts them on entering the background or locking; `Audio` sessions have no such restriction ([`MediaSessionManageriOS`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/ios/MediaSessionManagerIOS.mm), [`MediaSessionManagerInterface::applicationDidEnterBackground`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/MediaSessionManagerInterface.cpp)). An element is classified by `HTMLMediaElement::mediaType` from its tag before metadata and from whether the media has video after. Use `<audio>` and serve a track with audio frames only. (Whether an embedded ID3 picture counts as video in AVFoundation was not checked; keep artwork out of the file.)
- **Silent playback when the page becomes hidden, on iOS.** When visibility changes to hidden, WebKit interrupts any element that is playing but not audibly (muted, volume 0, or no audio) ([`MediaElementSession::visibilityChanged`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/d1cdb930fee370bcddb8407590b5f113ceaf1e89/Source/WebCore/html/MediaElementSession.cpp)), and a page that is not audible loses its process assertion (§3). Never start, unlock or keep playing an element muted or at volume 0.
- **Muted audio in a hidden page, on Chrome.** When the `kPauseMutedBackgroundAudio` feature is enabled (its default was not checked), Chrome pauses hidden audio at volume 0 ([`WebMediaPlayerImpl::ShouldPausePlaybackWhenHidden`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/c160f0f057a2c006a69c08875d4fe3c925c74e1b/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/web_media_player_impl.cc)); unmuted audio-only playback is explicitly allowed in the background.
- **Any moment without audible output that needs script to continue.** Switching sources at an `ended` event, playing silence between items, or waiting for a fetch between segments all depend on script running in a hidden page, which iOS stops when audio stops (§3) and which iOS releases have repeatedly broken ([bug 173332](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=173332), [bug 261554](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261554), and for home-screen apps [bug 261858](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261858)). One element playing one continuous file has no such moment until the track ends.
- **Running out of buffered data because a request fails** (§2). On Chrome this happens 2–5½ minutes after a failing request, not 10–20 seconds; on iOS the timing is unknown.

Inference, not verified: the reported pause 10–20 seconds after locking fits Cup's current design, where the next segment starts on the standby element from an `ended` handler, the standby element was started muted to unlock it, and a paused standby element in a hidden page gets a purgeable buffering policy. If the pause coincides with the first segment boundary after locking, the single-element design removes its cause.

## Answers the spec depends on

Confirmed:

- Chrome reads one right-unbounded range at a time and stays only 2–5½ minutes ahead of a 128 kbps track; it never downloads a 30–90 minute track early.
- Every request both browsers make carries the cookie valid at that moment; no page script is involved. Media authorization must stay valid from Play until the track ends and across pauses, not only at playback start.
- Chrome fails a `401` immediately and retries transport failures for about 30 seconds; the element does not recover from a fatal error on its own.
- While audio plays, page script runs on both platforms with timers at about 1-second alignment or better; once audio stops on iOS, the process can be suspended and nothing runs.
- Without handlers, iOS offers play/pause, a scrubber and ±15 s skips; Android offers play/pause and a seek bar. Setting only metadata changes title and artwork, nothing else.
- To avoid locked pauses: use `<audio>`, an audio-only file, never muted or silent playback, and no script-driven transitions during playback.

Unknown:

- How far ahead, and in what ranges, iOS AVFoundation reads a long progressive MP3, locked or not.
- Whether Chrome's paused-reading connection survives minutes of deferral through Cloudflare, i.e. whether one uninterrupted listen needs more than one request.
- How iOS reacts to a `401` mid-playback.
- Whether Android Chrome's default seek forward/backward buttons do anything without handlers.

## Experiments that would settle the unknowns

1. **Request log.** Serve a 60-minute 128 kbps CBR MP3 from the production Worker setup behind the media cookie and log every request: time, `Range`, the cookie's expiry and the response status (a test-only `console.log` read through `wrangler tail` is enough). On an iPhone in Safari and on an Android phone in Chrome, press Play, lock the device within 10 seconds and leave it locked for 65 minutes. Repeat once on cellular and once with a 5-minute lock-screen pause in the middle. The log answers how far ahead each browser reads, how many requests one listen takes, and whether any request arrives after the cookie expired.
2. **Expiry.** Repeat on each device with the cookie's `Max-Age` cut to 5 minutes and the JWT check unchanged. Record when playback stops relative to the first `401` and which element events fire (log `error`, `stalled` and `waiting` through `navigator.sendBeacon`, which also shows whether script ran).
3. **Controls.** With only `navigator.mediaSession.metadata` set, photograph the iOS lock screen and the Android notification and system media controls; press each button and note whether the position moves.

## Implications for the map

- The authorization ticket must choose a media credential whose lifetime covers a whole track plus pauses (or that is renewed without page script), because both browsers fetch throughout playback.
- The player ticket can keep the defaults on iOS; on Android it should decide whether to add `seekforward` and `seekbackward` handlers once experiment 3 confirms the buttons are inert, and whether a `play` handler is needed to refresh the credential before a lock-screen resume.
- The segment bitrate question matters little for Chrome, whose read-ahead is fixed in bytes; at 128 kbps a 2 MiB minimum is already over 2 minutes of audio.
