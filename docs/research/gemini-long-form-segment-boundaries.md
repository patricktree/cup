# How others make long-form Gemini narration flow across segment boundaries

> Historical research, incorporated 2026-11-14. It is evidence for [ADR 0027](../adr/0027-flow-narration-across-segment-boundaries-with-overlap-and-cut.md), which built overlap and cut as speech policy 7 in [#336](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/pull/336), deployed on 2026-11-13 without any of the cost levers this page names: no grouping of narration chunks, no Batch API and no seed. [ADR 0028](../adr/0028-withdraw-overlap-and-cut.md) withdrew it in [#352](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/pull/352), merged and deployed on 2026-11-14, because Whisper’s word timing put the cut point in the wrong place. The effort continues on the map [Map: decouple speech synthesis from synchronization units](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/320), which synthesizes larger pieces than one synchronization unit; this page’s finding that fresh starts also occur inside one long request, at internal segments of about 290 words, bears on how large those pieces can be. [Original research](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/01cf19d87291687133ee3c1a64c2b65e15d88ddb/docs/research/gemini-long-form-segment-boundaries.md) on the branch `research/gemini-long-form-narration-seams`.

Researched on 2026-11-13 for [How do others make long-form Gemini narration flow across segment boundaries?](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/319), part of the map [Map: narration flows across segment boundaries](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/288). Cup synthesizes each narration chunk on its own with `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` (voice Charon) through Cloudflare AI Gateway's Interactions API ([`synthesize-gemini-audio.ts`](../../libs/audiobook-production/src/synthesize-gemini-audio.ts)) and joins the segment MP3s byte for byte; the boundaries sound like a fresh start. This note looks outward: what Google offers for long-form Gemini speech, what other users of Gemini TTS report and do, and which techniques of other providers transfer. It builds on [What can Gemini 3.8 TTS condition on without speaking it?](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/289) (findings in [Gemini TTS unspoken context](gemini-tts-unspoken-context.md)) and on [Which approach makes segment boundaries inaudible?](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/291), whose blind test chose overlap and cut at about 1.62 times the synthesized audio. Aligners for overlap and cut are a separate ticket and not covered here.

Each claim is marked **documented** (by Google or the named provider, in its own docs), **reported** (by users, in public threads or code), **measured** (in this note's probes) or **unverified**.

**Short answer:** no route found reaches overlap and cut's continuity for less extra audio. Google documents no long-form or continuity mechanism for Gemini 3.8 TTS: the Cloud Text-to-Speech API does not serve the 3.8 models at all, its long audio synthesis does not mention Gemini voices, and its streaming still synthesizes one input after the client half-closes. Users of Gemini TTS report the same fresh starts Cup hears, between requests and also inside one long request, and report no fix: they regenerate takes, join at paragraph pauses with silence, or move to ElevenLabs. One user traced in-request steps to internal segments of about 290 words that Google runs inside one request; a 1,003-word Flash-Lite request in this note shows loudness plateaus that change at about the same spacing. So longer requests do not remove fresh starts; past roughly 290 words they move them to where Cup cannot cut. The continuity features of other providers (Cartesia continuations, ElevenLabs request stitching) all condition on the previous generation's audio or state, which Gemini refuses, and are serial. The cheaper lever is inside overlap and cut itself: its overhead is per boundary, and Cup's narration chunks are short, so grouping consecutive narration chunks into requests of up to about 2,000 characters would cut the extra text from ×1.46–2.03 to ×1.04–1.25 on the eval fixtures, at the price of audio segments that no longer map one to one onto synchronization units. A decoding `seed` makes a retried request reproduce its take, which matters for retried segments under overlap and cut.

## Community reports

What users of Gemini TTS report, all **reported**, none confirmed by Google:

- **Fresh starts between requests, as in Cup.** Splitting a chapter gave two halves that were "very different", one "more rumbling and aggressive" ([2.5 Pro thread, 2025-09-24](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/inconsistent-audio-output-with-gemini-2-5-pro-preview-tts/101454)); 2,000-word chunks of a 20,000-word text sound "as if different voices or speaking styles are stitched" (same thread, 2025-09-28); same voice and configuration give "an audibly different voice on each run", so chunked audio does not match ([3.1 production thread, 2026-09-09](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-tts-gemini-3-1-flash-tts-in-production-voice-consistency-across-runs-long-input-cutoffs-accent-drift-emotional-range-guidance-roadmap/182191); [3.1 identity thread, 2026-08-24](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-1-flash-tts-preview-voice-identity-changes-between-separate-requests/179498)). Google staff asked for payloads in each thread and posted no workaround.
- **Fresh starts inside one request on 3.8.** The 3.8 thread reports abrupt tone changes "after a phrase change" in 2 to 7-minute single requests on Flash and Flash-Lite, a 5-minute single-request recording that changes speaker character around 1:04, about five drifts in 10 minutes, and a three-minute Spanish single request that changes at 2:09 ([3.8 thread](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-8-tts-voice-consistency-issue/186103), posts 1, 5, 7 and 21). One user switched from separate scene recordings to one full-script request "specifically to improve consistency" and still heard it. Google replied on 2026-10-15 that it is "looking into this issue"; as of 2026-11-08 no fix is posted, and a later post reports a further multi-speaker regression around 2026-11-05.
- **Internal segments.** One user (post 18, 2026-10-16, Flash 3.8 with a Voice Design voice) found steps that "line up with the internal segments reported in the usage data" (`model_invocation_token_counts`): a 2,019-word request "ran as 7 invocations, roughly 290 words each", each with 840 audio tokens of what they take to be the voice reference and later ones with more text tokens, "which suggests each one is also given some of the preceding text". The step falls "exactly on that first boundary", by 0.8 to 2.1 dB in 7 of 10 takes, and never in 12 short requests of the same passage. Their reading is that each internal segment "starts again from the voice reference with only the preceding text, not the preceding audio". `previous_interaction_id` was refused, and splitting into shorter requests "didn't help either, because the joins between requests sounded the same way".
- **Workarounds tried.** Regenerating until a take does not drift ("10 times or more", post 15); replacing periods with commas (more drift, post 1); multi-speaker mode with one narrator and different groupings (no help, post 1); fewer than 600 tokens per request ("works better", but variation remains, 2.5 Pro thread post 8); several takes per chunk, budgeted as $50 for a 100,000-word book (2.5 Pro thread post 21); and moving to ElevenLabs for consistency (3.8 thread posts 13 and 20).
- **Open-source pipelines.** GatelessGate joins Gemini clips with 300 ms of silence and argues "a clean cut plus a natural pause beats a crossfade" for speech ([`gemini-tts.js`](https://github.com/KilledByAPixel/GatelessGate/blob/main/scripts/lib/gemini-tts.js)). DubbLM's Gemini 3.8 wrapper passes "the previous translated line" to the model "as a quoted delivery context inside `speech_metadata.style` (never spoken)", up to 140 characters ([`gemini38_tts_wrapper.py`](https://github.com/ArteusAI/DubbLM/blob/main/src/tts/gemini38_tts_wrapper.py)), the variant Cup's blind test found no better than policy 6. Inworld's own long-text scripts split at paragraph, then sentence boundaries into 500 to 1,600-character chunks and join with configurable silence ([Inworld](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/long-text-input)). None of the code found overlaps and cuts.

No one found reports the fresh starts gone. The closest to a method is what Cup already does or tested: paragraph-boundary chunks, a pause at each join, a constant short style, and unspoken previous text in the style.

### Internal segments inside one request

**Measured, without listening.** One 1,003-word, 6,493-character English request (Anthropic fixture chunks 80–96) returned 396 s of audio in 36 s. Its usage reports only totals (1,299 text input tokens, 9,908 audio output tokens), no `model_invocation_token_counts` and no audio input tokens, so Flash-Lite through the gateway does not expose internal segments the way the user above saw on Flash. The speech level per paragraph (Whisper-located) sits on plateaus that change twice:

| Chunks | Words   | Mean speech level   |
| ------ | ------- | ------------------- |
| 80–84  | 1–272   | −18.5 to −17.8 dBFS |
| 85–87  | 273–588 | −16.9 to −16.3 dBFS |
| 88–93  | 589–829 | −19.7 to −19.4 dBFS |
| 94–96  | 830–    | −18.9 to −17.1 dBFS |

The first change, +5.9 dB across the start of chunk 85 (a section heading), and the second, around word 589, are about 300 words apart, the spacing the user reported. A heading also changes delivery, so this is consistent with internal segments on Flash-Lite but does not prove them. If they exist, a request longer than about 290 words (about 1,800 English characters) gets fresh starts Cup cannot see, align or cut, which is why longer requests are not a route to fewer fresh starts. The 1,600-character continuous references of the boundary diagnosis stayed under that length.

Every documented continuity mechanism conditions the next generation on the previous one's audio or decoder state, and each makes synthesis serial. Gemini 3.8 accepts text only ("TTS models accept text-only inputs", [limitations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#limitations)), so none of them can be borrowed, and serial synthesis is out of scope for the map anyway. Overlap and cut is the independent-request equivalent: the model hears its neighbour's edge in its own audio and the cut throws it away.

## Seed

**Measured:** two requests with the same text (chunk 80, 313 characters) and `generation_config.seed: 42` both returned 20.68 s of audio; their 20 ms loudness envelopes correlate at 0.994, against 0.230 for an unseeded take of the same text (19.92 s). The samples are not identical (sample correlation 0.51), so the seed fixes the decoded take while the waveform differs in detail. So a seed makes a retried request reproduce nearly the same audio, including its pauses, which would let a retried audio segment under overlap and cut land its cut in the same place and sound like the take it replaces. Whether one shared seed makes different texts sound more like one take (timbre or energy) is **unverified**; it is cheap to try in a listening test.

## Cost of the routes

Audio output is about 99% of a segment's synthesis cost ([unspoken-context findings](gemini-tts-unspoken-context.md)), so extra spoken text is the cost. The prototype measured ×1.62 in audio on its 13 segments, in line with the "today" column. Grouping to about 2,000 characters keeps each request under the 290-word internal segment length reported above; 4,000 would cross it. What grouping would cost besides audio:

- An audio segment would cover several narration chunks, while today each synchronization unit has its own audio segment; unit offsets inside a grouped segment would come from the aligner that overlap and cut needs anyway.
- A failed or retried request re-synthesizes more audio, and fewer, longer requests run less in parallel; the 6,493-character request took 36 s against the 90 s request timeout.
- Within a group, the boundaries are the model's own paragraph transitions, which the boundary diagnosis used as its continuous reference; heading boundaries inside a group were not judged separately.

## Conclusion for the overlap cost ticket

For [How much overlap, and at what cost?](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/317): no Google route, community practice or other provider's technique reaches overlap and cut's continuity with independent requests for less extra audio. Google offers no long-form mechanism for 3.8 beyond one request of up to about 655 s, and users report that one long request has its own fresh starts. The cost levers left are within overlap and cut: speaking less than a full edge sentence, grouping short narration chunks into requests of up to about 2,000 characters (×1.04–1.25 instead of ×1.46–2.03, with the synchronization-unit change above), the Batch API's half price if its latency fits, and a seed to make retries reproducible. Note that Google's price for Flash-Lite TTS audio doubles on 2027-01-01, from $6 to $12 per million audio tokens ([pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing#gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts)).

The [probe archive](./gemini-long-form-segment-boundaries.tar.xz) contains `long-request.mjs`, `long-stream.mjs` and `seed-request.mjs` (paid synthesis), `paragraph-steps.mjs` (paid Whisper pass and paragraph measurements), `steps.mjs` (level across every pause), `overlap-factor.mjs` (the cost table, free), one JSON record per request with audio redacted, the Whisper words and the MP3s. Unpack and rerun from the repository root:

```sh
tar -xJf docs/research/gemini-long-form-segment-boundaries.tar.xz -C /tmp
node /tmp/gemini-long-form-segment-boundaries/overlap-factor.mjs .
node /tmp/gemini-long-form-segment-boundaries/long-request.mjs apps/cloudflare-worker/.env.local . /tmp/boundaries-out 80 96 long-80-96
```

The synthesis scripts write raw PCM; encode it with `ffmpeg -f s16le -ar 24000 -ac 1 -i <label>.pcm <label>.mp3` before running `paragraph-steps.mjs <env-file> <out-dir> <label>`.
