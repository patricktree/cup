# Animated transitions between Recents and the detail view

> 2026-10-29: only the cover morph was built, in both directions, on the loading model of [#143](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/143); see [cover morph](../architecture/web-app.md#routes-and-navigation). The grid reflow and the title morph were dropped. Unlike the recommendation below, the router's `types` function names the covers through a ref registry rather than CSS custom properties, browsers without view transition types get no transition, and Playwright keeps its default motion setting: the one story that goes Back before its tests tap waits for the morph to finish.

Research date: 2026-10-29. This is a feasibility study; nothing is built. Repository observations describe `main` at [`9c02fef`](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/tree/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1); code links below are permalinks to that commit. Installed library sources are `@tanstack/react-router` 1.170.42, `@tanstack/router-core` 1.171.35 and `react`/`react-dom` 19.3.0, as resolved in [`pnpm-lock.yaml`](../../pnpm-lock.yaml). Browser support is `@mdn/browser-compat-data` 8.1.7 (published 2026-10-22). Specifications are CSS View Transitions Level 1 (Editor's Draft, 2025-02-16) and Level 2 (Editor's Draft, 2026-08-31). `@formkit/auto-animate` is 0.10.0, the npm `latest` on the research date. No prototype was run, and no browser or device was measured; behaviour marked as unverified needs a prototype.

## Question

Can the web app easily get three animations? (1) On Recents, when a card is added or removed, the other cards move to their new places (FLIP-style reflow). (2) Pressing a card morphs its cover image and title into their places on the detail view (a shared-element transition). (3) Going back from the detail view morphs the cover and title back into the card.

## Answer

- **(1) Grid reflow: moderate, about 1–2 days.** Use a same-document View Transition around the Recents list update, with a `view-transition-name` on each card and a shared `view-transition-class`. FLIP libraries (AutoAnimate, Motion's `layout`) fight the masonry hook: they measure the new positions before its `ResizeObserver` has set the new row spans. Query data arrives outside React transitions, so the update needs an explicit `document.startViewTransition` plus `flushSync`. Most effort goes into deciding which changes to animate: polls change card heights every 5 seconds, which reshuffles the masonry.
- **(2) Card to detail: moderate, about 1–2 days.** TanStack Router already wraps navigations in `document.startViewTransition` when `viewTransition` or `defaultViewTransition` is set, so the morph itself is CSS. Three things in Cup stand in the way. The detail view suspends on its first render, so the transition would capture the loading fallback. `DSCoverImage` hides every image until its `load` event, so the transition would capture the grey placeholder. And the detail title lives inside the narration document's injected HTML, so it can only be named imperatively.
- **(3) Detail back to card: moderate to hard, about 1–2 days on top of (2).** The morph mechanics are the same, but the in-app Back control pushes a new `/recent` entry. The router then scrolls Recents to the top instead of restoring its position, so the target card can be off screen. Every card's cover also remounts as a placeholder, deletion needs its own path, and the iOS swipe-back gesture may need the transition skipped.
- **Recommended approach:** plain browser View Transitions, driven by the router for (2) and (3) and by a small Recents hook for (1). Give an element a transition name only while its transition runs, and turn the animations off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. React 19.3's `<ViewTransition>` is now stable and is the alternative. It fits (1) less well, because query updates do not activate it, and it cannot wrap the injected detail title.
- **Browser support is broad enough for progressive enhancement.** Same-document transitions work in Chrome 111, Firefox 144 and Safari 18. View transition types work in Chrome 125, Firefox 147 and Safari 18.2. iOS shells from iOS 15 to 17 get no animation, which is harmless.

## Relation to the loading map

> Note, 2026-10-30: the map was built in [#165](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/pull/165) and recorded in [ADR 0016](../adr/0016-keep-the-query-cache-for-the-page-lifetime-and-load-screens-in-route-loaders.md), so the prerequisites below now exist. No transition is built, and the items under "Still open after the map" remain open.

The map [Navigate the web app without loading flashes](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/143) leaves View Transitions out of scope, but several of its decisions remove prerequisites that this page lists. Once they are built, the transition work shrinks to the pieces that are specific to animation.

- **Detail data before the transition:** [Where a screen starts loading its data](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/147) gives every route a loader that awaits `queryClient.query({ ...options, staleTime: 'static' })` and has components read through suspense hooks. That replaces step 2 of (2), including its `ensureQueryData` call, which TanStack Query 5.104 deprecates ([research on loaders and Query](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/144)).
- **Data still cached on return:** [Cache lifetime policy](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/146) sets `gcTime: Infinity` for the page lifetime, so the `gcTime: 0` concern in (2) and the open questions goes away, and Recents renders its cards on the first frame after Back.
- **Back keeps Recents' scroll position:** [Whether the detail view's Back control returns to Recents' scroll position](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/152) makes Back go back in history when Recents is the entry below. That covers step 1 of (3). A deletion also goes back in history in that case, so step 4 of (3) has to hold for a `popstate` as well as a `replace`.
- **Preloading and pending timing:** [the first-visit prototype](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/148) switches preloading to `viewport` and sets `pendingMs` 500 and `pendingMinMs` 300, with a top progress bar after 100 ms. The router starts the transition only after loaders finish, so on a slow first visit the listener sees the progress bar and then the morph. Whether the progress bar should be named out of the transition (`view-transition-name: none`) needs a prototype.
- **Detail view loading markers:** [Which tests guard against a loading flash](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/156) splits the detail view's loading state from "Preparing track". The morph must target the loaded content, so a track that is still being prepared should get no shared names.

Still open after the map: the `DSCoverImage` first-frame fix, naming the injected title, skipping the transition after an iOS swipe-back, choosing which Recents changes animate, and reduced motion in the app and in the Playwright configs.

## Platform facts

### Same-document View Transitions

- `document.startViewTransition(update)` captures the old state, runs `update` asynchronously, and captures the new state once the promise it returns has settled; rendering is suppressed in between ([CSS View Transitions 1, §6.1, §7.3–7.4](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-view-transitions-1/)). While rendering is suppressed, pointer hit testing targets the document element ([§7.1.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-view-transitions-1/)).
- If two rendered elements share a `view-transition-name` when a state is captured, the transition is skipped (`InvalidStateError`), but the update still runs ([§7.3.1–7.3.2, §1.4](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-view-transitions-1/); [MDN, Using the View Transition API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API/Using)).
- The snapshots are replaced content, so `object-fit` and `object-position` apply to them, and width and height changes are animated as a smooth scale ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API/Using)). For text that rewraps, the Chrome guidance is `width: fit-content` on both sides, or `height: 100%` on the old and new snapshots ([modern-web-guidance `same-document-transitions`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/modern-web-guidance), retrieved 2026-10-29).
- Level 2 adds `view-transition-class`, which styles many uniquely named groups with one `::view-transition-group(.class)` rule. It also adds `startViewTransition({ update, types })` with `:active-view-transition-type()`, `match-element`, and element-scoped transitions ([CSS View Transitions 2, §2.2–2.3, §3.1, §4, §10.3](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-view-transitions-2/)). Cross-document transitions (`@view-transition`) do not apply to this SPA.
- In the HTML event loop's _update the rendering_, resize observations are broadcast before "perform pending transition operations" ([HTML, update the rendering](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/webappapis.html#update-the-rendering)). A row span that `useMasonryRowSpans` writes in its `ResizeObserver` callback is therefore part of the captured new state.
- `PopStateEvent.hasUAVisualTransition` is `true` when the browser already animated a history navigation, for example after a swipe-back gesture. It exists so that pages can skip their own transition in that case ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PopStateEvent/hasUAVisualTransition)).

### TanStack Router

- With `viewTransition` on a navigation, or `defaultViewTransition` on the router, the router calls `document.startViewTransition`. Given `{ types }`, it passes them only where `CSS.supports("selector(:active-view-transition-type(a))")` holds and otherwise falls back to a plain callback. A `types` function receives `{ fromLocation, toLocation, pathChanged, … }` and can return `false` to skip the transition ([router-core `src/router.ts` L301–L309, L898–L908, L2432–L2481](https://github.com/TanStack/router/blob/%40tanstack/router-core%401.171.35/packages/router-core/src/router.ts); [`src/link.ts` L326–L338](https://github.com/TanStack/router/blob/%40tanstack/router-core%401.171.35/packages/router-core/src/link.ts); [RouterOptions docs](https://tanstack.com/router/latest/docs/framework/react/api/router/RouterOptionsType)).
- A navigation sets the per-navigation flag; a `popstate`, such as browser Back or Android back, reaches `router.load` with no flag, so `defaultViewTransition` applies to it (`router.ts` L2307 and L2435–L2436; [`Transitioner.tsx` L41](https://github.com/TanStack/router/blob/%40tanstack/react-router%401.170.42/packages/react-router/src/Transitioner.tsx)).
- Loaders run before the transition starts. Inside the update callback, the router commits through `React.startTransition`, waits until React has rendered the new matches (acknowledged in a layout effect), and emits `onRendered`, which runs scroll restoration ([router-core `src/load-client.ts` L1864–L1938](https://github.com/TanStack/router/blob/%40tanstack/router-core%401.171.35/packages/router-core/src/load-client.ts); [react-router `Transitioner.tsx` L31–L37, `Matches.tsx` L96–L100](https://github.com/TanStack/router/blob/%40tanstack/react-router%401.170.42/packages/react-router/src/Matches.tsx); [router-core `src/scroll-restoration.ts` L250–L370](https://github.com/TanStack/router/blob/%40tanstack/router-core%401.171.35/packages/router-core/src/scroll-restoration.ts)). The new snapshot therefore includes the committed route and the restored scroll position.
- The scroll restoration key defaults to the history entry's `__TSR_key`, falling back to the `href` (`scroll-restoration.ts` L49–L51). A pushed `/recent` is a new entry with no saved position, so it scrolls to the top. The key is configurable with `getScrollRestorationKey` (`router.ts` L494).
- A newly mounted `<Suspense>` boundary shows its fallback even inside a React transition. The detail view's boundary is new on every navigation from Recents, so without data in the cache the new snapshot is the "preparing" fallback. This follows from the code above; the React docs say the same for shared elements ([React `<ViewTransition>`](https://react.dev/reference/react/ViewTransition)).

### React 19.3 `<ViewTransition>`

- `<ViewTransition>` and `addTransitionType` are stable in React 19.3.0, released 2026-09-09 ([React changelog](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md#1930-september-9-2026)), and the installed `react@19.3.0` exports both.
- It activates only for updates inside a Transition, a `<Suspense>` reveal or `useDeferredValue`; a plain `setState` does not activate it, and a `flushSync` in between skips it. React calls `document.startViewTransition` itself and interrupts any other View Transition on the page. Shared elements use a `name` that must be unique among mounted components, and no pair forms when either side is outside the viewport or a Suspense fallback shows in between. React skips animations for synchronous `popstate` updates, and it does not disable animations under reduced motion ([React `<ViewTransition>` reference](https://react.dev/reference/react/ViewTransition)).
- TanStack Query delivers results through `useSyncExternalStore`, whose updates are synchronous, so a history poll or invalidation would not activate `<ViewTransition>` unless the list renders from `useDeferredValue` (inferred from the activation rule; unverified).

### FLIP libraries

- **AutoAnimate** animates additions, removals and moves of a parent's direct children, sets `position: relative` on a static parent, and turns itself off under `prefers-reduced-motion` ([AutoAnimate docs](https://auto-animate.formkit.com/)). Version 0.10.0 measures new positions with `getBoundingClientRect()` in its `MutationObserver` callback, which runs before resize observations are delivered (`index.mjs`, functions `remain` and `getCoords`). For Cup's masonry this means a new card is measured with no row span yet, and so are the cards it pushes down. To animate a removal, AutoAnimate also puts the removed node back into the list, where the masonry's `MutationObserver` sees it again.
- **Motion** `layout` animates layout changes from a React render with transforms. `layoutId` pairs elements across components, `AnimatePresence` keeps exiting elements mounted, and images or text that change aspect ratio do better with `layout="position"` ([Motion layout animations](https://motion.dev/docs/react-layout-animations)). It would mean a new dependency, `motion.*` wrappers on every card, cover and title, and the same measuring order problem as AutoAnimate. It cannot wrap the detail title in the injected HTML either.

## Recommended approach

### Shared pieces

- **Name elements only while their transition runs.** With permanent names on all of Recents' cards, every navigation, such as opening the Add or account dialog, would capture up to 50 named groups, and the mini player's cover could duplicate a name. Set names through a per-element custom property, for example `style={{ "--vt-cover": "cover-" + id }}`, and apply them only under a transition-scoped class or type, for example `:root:active-view-transition-type(open-track) [data-vt-open] { view-transition-name: var(--vt-cover) }`. For the clicked card alone, set the name directly before navigating, as in the "dynamic list item" pattern ([modern-web-guidance `same-document-transitions`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/modern-web-guidance)).
- **Reduced motion.** Add `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { ::view-transition-group(*), ::view-transition-old(*), ::view-transition-new(*) { animation: none !important; } }` to the global styles, or skip the transition (the router's `types` function returns `false`, and the Recents hook does not call `startViewTransition`). Skipping also avoids the frame in which hit testing goes to the document element.
- **Covers that are already cached must render on the first frame.** `DSCoverImage` should show an image that has already loaded right away, for example by checking `img.complete` in a layout effect or ref callback, which re-renders before paint. Otherwise both morphs end on the placeholder and the cover pops in afterwards. This also removes the cover pop-in on every Recents remount today.
- **Keep the root cross-fade short,** or drop it with `:root { view-transition-name: none }` for the list transition, so the sticky mini player and tab bar do not fade ([modern-web-guidance `group-element-transitions`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/modern-web-guidance)).
- Use plain View Transitions rather than React `<ViewTransition>`. Do not mix the two: React interrupts View Transitions it did not start.

### (1) Grid reflow

1. In `RecentPage`, render the cards from a `shownItems` state instead of the query's `items` directly ([`_shell.recent.tsx` L46, L151–L153](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/routes/_shell.recent.tsx#L46-L153)). When the set of `conversionId`s changes, other than by an appended page, call `document.startViewTransition(() => flushSync(() => setShownItems(items)))` behind a feature and reduced-motion check; otherwise set the state directly.
2. Give each `<li>` in `RecentsCard` a unique name (`card-${conversionId}`) and `view-transition-class: recents-card` under a class set on the root for the list transition, and style `::view-transition-group(.recents-card)` for the move. Use `::view-transition-new(.recents-card):only-child` and `::view-transition-old(.recents-card):only-child` for the entering and leaving card ([modern-web-guidance `group-element-transitions`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/modern-web-guidance)).
3. The masonry spans need no change: the new spans are written before the new state is captured (see the HTML ordering above).
4. Make the changes visible. Close the Add dialog before the history refetch lists the new card, so the card is not added behind the dialog's backdrop ([`account.ts` L75–L80](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/data-fetching/account.ts#L75-L80)). After a deletion, remove the item from the cached history before navigating to Recents ([`reader.ts` L98–L102](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/data-fetching/reader.ts#L98-L102)); see (3) for how the deleted card can leave visibly.
5. Decide whether height changes from polls animate too. They reshuffle most of the grid, and a document-wide transition every 5 seconds would briefly block taps and freeze the mini player's progress in a snapshot ([modern-web-guidance](https://www.npmjs.com/package/modern-web-guidance): "any animations will appear to be paused"). The recommendation is to animate additions and removals only at first.

AutoAnimate or Motion would be the quick route on a plain grid, but here they would need the masonry spans computed synchronously in a layout effect before they measure. That rewrites the hook and still leaves AutoAnimate's re-inserted removed node to handle.

### (2) Card to detail

1. Turn on transitions for this navigation only: `viewTransition={{ types: ["open-track"] }}` on the card's `Link` ([`recents-card.tsx` L42–L44](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/app/components/recents-card.tsx#L42-L44)), or a router-wide `defaultViewTransition` whose `types` function returns `false` for every other pair of locations. Where types are unsupported, the router falls back to a plain transition, so names must not depend only on the type selector there (for example, also set a class on the root in the card's `onClick`).
2. Add a route `loader` that awaits `context.queryClient.ensureQueryData(createAudiobookQuery(conversionId))`, so the router waits for the data before the transition starts and the detail view renders its content, not its fallback, in the new state ([`reader.ts` L19–L30](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/data-fetching/reader.ts#L19-L30)). `defaultPreload: "intent"` then also prefetches the audiobook on hover or touch. With `gcTime: 0`, the loaded query has no observer until the component mounts, so give the audiobook query a short non-zero `gcTime` and verify that the data survives until the component mounts.
3. Name the card's cover and title (`cover-${id}`, `title-${id}`) for this transition. On the detail view, give the `DSCoverImage` the same cover name. The title is injected HTML, so set `title.style.viewTransitionName` in the existing `applyHeadingTextStyles` layout effect ([`_shell.audiobooks.$conversionId.tsx` L496–L515](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/routes/_shell.audiobooks.%24conversionId.tsx#L496-L515)). Layout effects run before the router's acknowledgement, so the name is there when the new state is captured.
4. Fit the snapshots. Both covers are 5:3, so the cover only scales and moves. The card's cover has square corners and gets its rounded bottom corners only from the card's clip, which the snapshot does not carry, and the detail cover is rounded on all corners. Expect a short corner mismatch, or round the card cover's bottom corners itself. The title changes from h3 to h2 and rewraps, so use `width: fit-content` on both sides or a pure cross-fade for the title group instead of a stretched scale.
5. Focus: after navigation the detail view keeps the router's default focus handling. Check that focus does not stay on the vanished card ([modern-web-guidance](https://www.npmjs.com/package/modern-web-guidance) asks for focus to move to the new heading).

### (3) Detail back to card

1. Make Back return to the same scroll position. Either make the Back control call `router.history.back()` when the previous entry is Recents (as the dialog close hook already does), or key Recents' scroll restoration by path with `getScrollRestorationKey`. Without this, in-app Back scrolls Recents to the top today, and the morph would fly to a card off screen.
2. Name the detail cover and title for the back transition, and the matching card's cover and title in the new state. The card component can read the conversion it comes back from, for example from router state or a module-level "last opened" id. The history query is still cached from the detail view, so the cards render on the first frame, and with the cover fix their covers do too.
3. Browser Back and Android back are `popstate` and get `defaultViewTransition`. Skip the transition when `PopStateEvent.hasUAVisualTransition` is `true`, such as after an iOS Safari swipe-back. The router does not pass the event to `types`, so a capture-phase `popstate` listener has to record the flag for the `types` function.
4. Deletion navigates with `replace` to Recents. Give that navigation no shared names, or `viewTransition: false`, and let (1) animate the card out once the cached history no longer lists it.

## Risks and test impact

- **Snapshot of the wrong state.** The Suspense fallback (detail view) and the cover placeholder (both screens) are the biggest risks. Each would make the morph land on the wrong content and is easy to miss on a fast development machine. Both need the code changes above, not only CSS.
- **Interaction during transitions.** While a document transition runs, taps go to the document element, so a tap during the roughly 250–500 ms animation is lost. This matters most for (1) if polls trigger transitions.
- **Duplicate names skip the whole transition.** One duplicate, for example a cover named on both the card and the mini player, silently disables the animation. Name elements only for the running transition.
- **Masonry reshuffles.** An entering or leaving card near the top moves most cards to another column, so the reflow is busy by nature. Keep it short.
- **WebKit is untested.** Playwright runs Chromium only, and iOS Safari 18 and later need a manual check, including the swipe-back interaction.
- **Screenshot tests.** Playwright's `toHaveScreenshot` disables CSS animations, transitions and Web Animations by default and waits for two identical screenshots in a row ([Playwright `toHaveScreenshot`](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-pageassertions#page-assertions-to-have-screenshot-1)). The docs do not say whether that covers `::view-transition` pseudo-element animations. To stay deterministic, set `use: { reducedMotion: "reduce" }` in both Playwright configs ([Playwright `reducedMotion`](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-testoptions#test-options-reduced-motion)) and make reduced motion skip or flatten the transitions, then add one targeted test that runs with `no-preference` and checks that `document.startViewTransition` is called and the shared names are unique.
- **Layout-based tests stay valid.** View Transitions animate snapshots, not the live DOM, so the masonry box tests read final positions. With AutoAnimate or Motion they would read mid-animation transforms.
- **Click tests may get slower.** During the transition, Playwright's actionability check sees the document element at the click point and retries, so the routing E2E test's Back click waits for the animation. That makes the test slower, not flaky.

## Open questions

- Which changes on Recents should animate: additions and removals only, or also polls that change card heights?
- Should the title morph at all, given the order flip (title above cover on the card, below it on the detail view) and the change in size? A cover-only morph with a cross-fade for the rest is cheaper and less fragile.
- How does the map's top progress bar, shown after 100 ms of a pending navigation, combine with the transition that starts once the loader finishes? Needs a prototype once the map is built. (The earlier question about `ensureQueryData` and `gcTime: 0` is settled by the map; see [Relation to the loading map](#relation-to-the-loading-map).)
- If `<ViewTransition>` is adopted later, does TanStack Router's asynchronous commit after `popstate` avoid React's popstate skip? Unverified.
- Should the mini player's cover, which also opens the detail view ([`mini-player.tsx` L44, L96](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/9c02fef4050a7c1e65c56df9c3235f6b6cceb4a1/apps/web-app/src/app/components/mini-player.tsx#L96)), morph too? Its circle crop would need a different treatment.
- Revisit element-scoped transitions (`ul.startViewTransition`) for (1) once Safari and Firefox ship them: they would keep the rest of Recents interactive during a reflow.
