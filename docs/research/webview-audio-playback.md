# Narration track playback in the Capacitor WebView

> Historical research, incorporated 2026-10-27 by [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-serve-one-narration-track-and-play-it-with-one-audio-element.md). Cup adopted its main conclusion: with one narration track, playback needs no JavaScript while locked, so the native players and `AccountMediaPlugin` were removed and the shells play through the web player, keeping iOS `UIBackgroundModes` `audio`. The Android `mediaPlayback` foreground service and native system controls it calls for are deferred to [Android and iOS apps that play the narration track](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/55). Its cookie analysis is historical: the [signed media URL](../architecture/authentication.md#signed-media-url) replaced the media cookie. Repository paths below describe `0fb4e71d` and may no longer exist. External claims have not been reverified.

[Original investigation](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/20b6bd6e4ebfcef739b695c655e37a6048530a5e/docs/research/webview-audio-playback.md).

Research date: 2026-10-27, for [issue #57](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/57) of the [narration track map](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/56). Repository observations are from `origin/main` at `0fb4e71d`. Capacitor source is the installed 8.5.3 (`@capacitor/android`, `@capacitor/ios`, `@capacitor/cli`). WebKit source is `WebKit/WebKit` `main` at `d1cdb930`, Chromium source is `chromium/chromium` `main` at `313c1303`, and MDN compatibility data is `mdn/browser-compat-data` `main` at `0dd88129`, all read on the research date. Shipped iOS and Android System WebView releases can lag `main`. No physical device was available, so nothing here was observed on a device.

## Question

Can an `<audio>` element inside Cup's Capacitor WebView play a long narration track reliably on Android and iOS, with the device locked and the app backgrounded, and what native configuration does that need? The ticket asks to challenge, not inherit, the earlier conclusion that native players were required, and to cover system media controls, `navigator.mediaSession`, and cookie-authorized byte-range requests.

## Answer

| Point                                            | iOS (`WKWebView`)                                                                                                                | Android (System WebView)                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Engine pauses audio-only media when backgrounded | No, confirmed in source: audio-only elements carry no background or lock restriction                                             | No, confirmed in source: "Audio only stream is allowed to play when in background"                                                            |
| Shell pauses the WebView                         | No, confirmed in Capacitor source                                                                                                | No, confirmed in Capacitor source (`KeepRunning` defaults to true, so `WebView.onPause()` is never called)                                    |
| App process stays running for a whole track      | Likely with the existing `audio` background mode; not confirmed on a device                                                      | Not without a foreground service: from Android 14, cached processes freeze 10 seconds after the activity stops                                |
| Native configuration needed                      | Keep `UIBackgroundModes` `audio` (already present); WebKit sets the playback audio session category itself                       | A `mediaPlayback` foreground service, started while the app is visible and held while playing                                                 |
| System media controls                            | WebKit publishes Now Playing for an audio element longer than 0.95 s; not confirmed on a device for Cup                          | None: WebView has no media notification and disables the Media Session API                                                                    |
| `navigator.mediaSession`                         | Enabled by default in WebKit                                                                                                     | Absent: disabled by a WebView command-line switch                                                                                             |
| Cookie-authorized range requests                 | Media loads go through WebKit's own networking, so cookie policy applies; Cup's cookies are cross-site there and likely not sent | Media loads go through Chromium's network stack and `CookieManager`; only the Android media cookie, installed as `SameSite=None`, can be sent |

The earlier native players were justified by work that no longer exists: JavaScript had to keep scheduling synthesis and swapping segments while the WebView was suspended. With one narration track, playback needs no JavaScript while locked. What remains platform-specific is keeping the Android process alive, showing system controls on Android, and authorizing the track's requests for its whole duration.

## Why native players were introduced

Commit `7f62af1a` (2026-10-05) added the native coordinators together with progressive synthesis. Its mobile README states that each coordinator "owns playback intent, passage selection, and bounded speech synthesis lookahead independently of the WebView", and [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-prepare-narration-before-player-controlled-synthesis.md) says that "Native Android and iOS engines own playback and scheduling independently of their WebViews" while "Browser and native-platform support requires implementation verification". No investigation in the repository measured WebView background playback; the decision rested on the need to run scheduling code in the background. Under [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-synthesize-the-whole-narration-document.md) synthesis no longer depends on the player, and a single track removes segment switching, so that reason is gone.

## Cup's shell configuration

- [`capacitor.config.ts`](../../apps/mobile-app/capacitor.config.ts) sets only `appStartPath`, `CapacitorHttp` enabled and the App plugin's back-button handling. The default `server.hostname` is `localhost`, `androidScheme` `https` and `iosScheme` `capacitor` (`@capacitor/cli` declarations), so the page origin is `https://localhost` on Android and `capacitor://localhost` on iOS. Media requests to `https://cup.audio` are therefore cross-site on both platforms.
- The [Android manifest](../../apps/mobile-app/android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml) declares `NarrationPlaybackService` with `foregroundServiceType="mediaPlayback"` and the `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK` and `WAKE_LOCK` permissions. `targetSdkVersion` and `compileSdkVersion` are 36, `minSdkVersion` 24 (`android/variables.gradle`).
- [`MainActivity.java`](../../apps/mobile-app/android/app/src/main/java/audio/cup/app/MainActivity.java) extends `BridgeActivity` without lifecycle overrides beyond `onNewIntent`, and registers `AccountMediaPlugin`, `NarrationPlayerPlugin`, `AndroidSharePlugin` and `AndroidAppLinksPlugin`.
- The iOS [`Info.plist`](../../apps/mobile-app/ios/App/App/Info.plist) declares `UIBackgroundModes` `audio`. The audio session is configured only by the native player (README, Playback section).

## iOS

### Playback while locked

Capacitor's `CAPBridgeViewController.webViewConfiguration(for:)` sets `allowsInlineMediaPlayback = true`, `allowsAirPlayForMediaPlayback = true` and `mediaTypesRequiringUserActionForPlayback = []`, and Capacitor iOS has no code that pauses the web view on backgrounding.

WebKit does not pause audio-only media when the app goes to the background or the device locks. `MediaSessionManageriOS::resetRestrictions()` adds `BackgroundProcessPlaybackRestricted` only for `Video`, `WebAudio` and `VideoAudio`, and `SuspendedUnderLockPlaybackRestricted` only for `VideoAudio` ([MediaSessionManagerIOS.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/ios/MediaSessionManagerIOS.mm)). An `<audio>` element with an MP3 is the unrestricted `Audio` type.

WebKit sets the audio session category itself. The GPU process enables `setShouldManageAudioSessionCategory(true)` on iOS ([GPUProcess.cpp](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebKit/GPUProcess/GPUProcess.cpp)), and `MediaSessionManagerCocoa::updateSessionState()` chooses `MediaPlayback` whenever audible audio or video plays ([MediaSessionManagerCocoa.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/cocoa/MediaSessionManagerCocoa.mm)), which `AudioSessionIOS` maps to `AVAudioSessionCategoryPlayback` ([AudioSessionIOS.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/ios/AudioSessionIOS.mm)). Apple's [media playback configuration guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/configuring-your-app-for-media-playback) says the default session silences app audio on a locked device, and that the playback category "means your app can play background audio if you're using the Audio, AirPlay, and Picture in Picture background mode". Cup already has that mode.

While audible media plays, WebKit takes a `MediaPlayback` process assertion for the GPU process, and for the app process only when the app holds the private `com.apple.runningboard.assertions.webkit` entitlement (`WebProcessPool::updateAudibleMediaAssertions()` in [WebProcessPool.cpp](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebKit/UIProcess/WebProcessPool.cpp)). A third-party app like Cup does not get that assertion, so keeping Cup's own process alive depends on the `audio` background mode. The "Failed to acquire RBS assertion 'WebKit Media Playback'" log that a Capacitor developer reports on the [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/740354) names that entitlement, which matches this assertion; current WebKit skips the attempt without the entitlement, and the reporter says audio still played.

Background `<audio>` in a `WKWebView` app is supported behavior that Apple has fixed when it broke: [WebKit bug 203293](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=203293), reported by a Capacitor maintainer with a sample app that enabled background audio, stopped audio shortly after backgrounding on iOS 13; a WebKit engineer said the fix was in other system components, and commenters confirmed it in iOS 14.0. A January 2024 forum report on iOS 17.2.1 says a _new_ audio element could not start playing while backgrounded ([thread 713084](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/713084)); one continuous track avoids starting playback in the background, which the element pair does at every segment boundary.

Unknown: whether the app process, and with it `CapacitorHttp` requests such as position saves, keeps running for a whole long track on current iOS. The WebContent and GPU processes hold their own assertions, so the page may keep running even if the app process is suspended, but bridge calls would then stall. No source settles which happens.

### System controls and `navigator.mediaSession`

WebKit enables the Media Session API by default for all clients (`MediaSessionEnabled`, status `mature`, `defaultValue: true` in [UnifiedWebPreferences.yaml](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WTF/Scripts/Preferences/UnifiedWebPreferences.yaml)), and MDN lists `webview_ios` as mirroring Safari, which added it in version 15. WebKit publishes Now Playing information through MediaRemote ([MediaSessionManagerCocoa.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/platform/audio/cocoa/MediaSessionManagerCocoa.mm)). `MediaElementSession::canShowControlsManager()` makes an audio element eligible when it has a source, no error, is not muted and lasts longer than 0.95 seconds, the "You've got mail!" threshold ([MediaElementSession.cpp](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/html/MediaElementSession.cpp)). Nothing in that code depends on the embedding app, so lock-screen controls should appear for the narration track with WebKit's defaults, and `navigator.mediaSession.metadata` should supply title and artwork. This is read from source and not observed for Cup.

## Android

### Capacitor does not pause the WebView

`BridgeActivity.onPause()` calls `Bridge.onPause()`, which notifies plugins and calls `MockCordovaWebViewImpl.handlePause(keepRunning)`. Only when `keepRunning` is false does it call `setPaused(true)`, which runs `webView.onPause()` and `webView.pauseTimers()`. `Bridge.shouldKeepRunning()` returns the Cordova preference `KeepRunning` with default `true`, and Cup sets no such preference, so Capacitor never pauses Cup's WebView or its JavaScript timers (`Bridge.java`, `BridgeActivity.java`, `cordova/MockCordovaWebViewImpl.java` in `@capacitor/android` 8.5.3).

Chromium keeps audio-only media playing in a hidden page: `WebMediaPlayerImpl::ShouldPausePlaybackWhenHidden()` returns false for an unmuted player without video, with the comment "Audio only stream is allowed to play when in background" ([web_media_player_impl.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/web_media_player_impl.cc)).

### The process freezes without a foreground service

With the screen locked, Cup's activity is stopped. Android's [process lifecycle guide](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/process-lifecycle) classifies a process holding only activities whose `onStop()` returned as cached, and says cached processes "should cease all work". The [cached apps freezer](https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/cached-apps-freezer) states: "App processes in the cached state are frozen 10 seconds after entering the cached state" on Android 14 and higher, and frozen threads "can't perform CPU work until unfrozen". A process with a foreground service is instead "visible" and "extremely important". Playing audio in the WebView is not listed as keeping a process out of the cached state. The [capacitor-media-session](https://github.com/jofr/capacitor-media-session) plugin, a community source, describes the same symptom: Android "will force your app to go to sleep even if audio is currently playing in the WebView", which it works around with a foreground service.

So the WebView path needs a foreground service of type `mediaPlayback` ([service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types): permission `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK`, no runtime prerequisites, no time limit listed). Apps targeting Android 12 or later cannot start one from the background, but may when the app "transitions from a user-visible state" or the user acts on its UI ([background start restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)), so starting it on Play works. Its notification is hidden without `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, but the service still runs, and "Notifications related to media sessions are exempt" ([notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)). Cup's manifest already declares the type and permissions. `WAKE_LOCK` is only needed if a wake lock is taken; whether Chromium's audio output keeps the CPU awake on its own while the screen is off is not documented. Media3's network wake mode, which the current player uses, does not apply to WebView loading.

Matching the observed symptom: the map reports the web player stopping 10 to 20 seconds after locking. The 10-second freeze delay is consistent with that for a WebView without a foreground service, but the report concerns the browser player, so it is not evidence for the WebView.

### System controls, `navigator.mediaSession` and audio focus

Android WebView has no media notification and no Media Session API. `aw_main_delegate.cc` appends `switches::kDisableMediaSessionAPI` with the comment "WebView does not support MediaSession API since there's no UI for media metadata and controls" ([aw_main_delegate.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/android_webview/lib/aw_main_delegate.cc)). MDN lists `MediaSession` for `webview_android` as not supported, tracked in [crbug.com/40611412](https://crbug.com/40611412). Lock-screen and notification controls therefore need native code: a `MediaSession` (platform or Media3) whose play, pause and seek commands call into the page, and whose state the page reports, as the community plugin above does.

Audio focus probably works without native code: Chromium's content layer creates an `AudioFocusDelegate` for every media session (`MediaSessionImpl::Initialize()` in [media_session_impl.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/content/browser/media/session/media_session_impl.cc)), and the switch above disables only the Blink API. Whether WebView then pauses for calls and ducks for notifications was not checked on a device.

## Authorizing the track's requests

### Cup's cookies are cross-site in the WebView

Cup's server issues `cup_media` with `SameSite=Strict` ([`account-auth.ts`](../../libs/api-server/src/http/account-auth.ts)) and the grant session cookie with `SameSite=Lax` ([`grant-session.ts`](../../libs/conversion-grants/src/domain/grant-session.ts)). Because the WebView page is `https://localhost` or `capacitor://localhost`, an `<audio src="https://cup.audio/api/files/…">` request is a cross-site subresource request, for which browsers do not send `Lax` or `Strict` cookies. On Android, [`AccountMediaPlugin`](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/0fb4e71d/apps/mobile-app/android/app/src/main/java/audio/cup/app/AccountMediaPlugin.java) works around this for accounts only: it enables third-party cookies for the WebView and installs its own copy of `cup_media` with `SameSite=None; Secure`. No equivalent exists for the trial grant cookie, and iOS installs no media cookie at all (`account-auth.ios.ts` re-exports the web no-op).

### Android cookie store

`CapacitorHttp` sends native requests through `HttpURLConnection`, and the `CapacitorCookies` plugin installs `CapacitorCookieManager` as the default `java.net.CookieHandler` on load. Its `put()` writes every `Set-Cookie` into `android.webkit.CookieManager`, and `get()` reads from it, so cookies from API responses land in the same store the WebView's media requests use (`plugin/CapacitorCookies.java`, `plugin/CapacitorCookieManager.java`). The plugin also calls `removeSessionCookies()` at load, which spares Cup's cookies because they carry `Max-Age`. Progressive media URLs load through `MultiBufferDataSource`, Blink's resource loading ([web_media_player_impl.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/third_party/blink/renderer/platform/media/web_media_player_impl.cc)), so the network stack's cookie rules, `setAcceptThirdPartyCookies` and `SameSite` apply to the range requests. Consequence: account tracks can work through `AccountMediaPlugin`; trial tracks would need the grant cookie to be `SameSite=None` or installed the same way.

### iOS cookie store

WebKit routes AVFoundation's media loads through its own networking: `MediaPlayerPrivateAVFoundationObjC::createAVAssetForURL()` replaces the asset's resource-loader `URLSession` with `WebCoreNSURLSession` over WebKit's media resource loader, and on iOS also passes the page's cookies for the URL as `AVURLAssetHTTPCookiesKey` ([MediaPlayerPrivateAVFoundationObjC.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/avfoundation/objc/MediaPlayerPrivateAVFoundationObjC.mm)). Media requests therefore follow WebKit's cookie policy. Intelligent Tracking Prevention "is enabled by default in all `WKWebView` applications" since iOS 14 ([App-Bound Domains](https://webkit.org/blog/10882/app-bound-domains/)), WebKit's default third-party cookie blocking mode is `All` (`NetworkSession.h`), and Safari blocks "cookies for cross-site resources" by default since iOS 13.4 ([Full Third-Party Cookie Blocking](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)). Capacitor syncs `HTTPCookieStorage` into `WKWebsiteDataStore.default().httpCookieStore` after native responses (`CapacitorCookieManager.syncCookiesToWebView()`), so the cookies are present in the store, but a request from `capacitor://localhost` to `cup.audio` would likely not carry them, whatever their `SameSite` value. This is inference from source and documentation, not observed. An app can only let users disable ITP in Settings via `NSCrossWebsiteTrackingUsageDescription`.

### Authorization must outlast the track

Neither engine downloads a long track at once: both issue further range requests while playing, so the credential must stay valid until the last byte is fetched. Cup's media cookie "expires no later than the token" ([private media access](../architecture/authentication.md#private-media-access)), and `AccountMediaPlugin` clamps `maxAge` to 300 seconds. The current web player renews the cookie before each segment preload, which a single track no longer provides. A track longer than the credential therefore needs renewal while locked, which requires the page and the bridge to run, or a credential that does not depend on WebView cookie policy, such as a short-lived signed URL whose lifetime covers the track. Choosing between them belongs to the authorization ticket.

## Device experiments

Each experiment needs a debug build of the shell with one `<audio>` element playing a narration track of at least 60 minutes from `https://cup.audio`, and the native player removed or unused.

1. **iOS, locked for the whole track.** Play, lock, wait past the end. Record whether audio is continuous, whether lock-screen controls show title, artwork, duration and working Pause, Play and scrubbing, and whether position saves sent through `CapacitorHttp` reach the server while locked (server logs). Repeat with a phone call and with headphones unplugged. Variant: remove `UIBackgroundModes` `audio` to see whether it is required.
2. **Android, locked, no foreground service.** Play, lock, and note when audio stops. On Android 14 or later, `adb shell dumpsys activity processes` shows whether the process is cached and frozen. This confirms or refutes the freezer explanation.
3. **Android, locked, with a `mediaPlayback` foreground service started on Play.** Same as 2 for the whole track; check for gaps, that the renderer process survives (`adb shell ps -A | grep sandboxed_process`), whether Spotify pauses when Cup plays (audio focus), and behavior during a call.
4. **Range authorization on both platforms.** Play a private account track and a trial track, seek near the end, and inspect the requests with Safari Web Inspector or Chrome remote debugging: whether `Range` requests carry the cookie, return `206`, and keep working after the cookie's `Max-Age` passes.

## Limits

- Nothing was run on a device; every behavioral statement comes from source, documentation or third-party reports.
- WebKit and Chromium were read at `main`. iOS ships WebKit with the OS and Android System WebView updates separately, so devices may run older code. iOS's RunningBoard and AVAudioSession policies are not open source; how they treat a `WKWebView` app's background audio was inferred from WebKit's side and from bug reports.
- Apple Developer Forums threads and the capacitor-media-session README are lower-trust sources, used only as corroboration or to illustrate reported symptoms.
- The Android statement that audio playback does not exempt a process from the cached state rests on the absence of such an exemption in the lifecycle and freezer documentation, not on an explicit statement.
