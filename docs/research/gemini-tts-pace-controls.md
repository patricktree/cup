# Pace controls for Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS

> Historical research, incorporated 2026-11-11 by [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-time-stretch-narration-audio-after-synthesis-with-sonic.md), which rests on its finding that Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS has no speaking-rate parameter. The listening prototype ([#266](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/266)) then measured the `speech_metadata.style` pace direction recommended below at ×1.09 to ×1.26, too uneven for a small, uniform factor, so Cup sends no pace direction: speech policy 6 time-stretches each audio segment by ×1.05 with Sonic after synthesis, as the [conversion architecture](../architecture/conversion.md#narration-language) describes. The library choice was revisited in the [time-stretch technology research](time-stretch-technology.md). External claims have not been reverified.

[Original investigation](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/035d15b29fb8001e14147572ac6e4418ae5d09fb/docs/research/gemini-tts-pace-controls.md).

Researched on 2026-11-11 for [#264](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/264), part of the map [#263: narrate a little faster](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/263). The question is how to make the narration audio that `gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` produces a little faster, as Cup calls it through Cloudflare AI Gateway's Interactions API in [`synthesize-gemini-audio.ts`](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/65043649/libs/audiobook-production/src/synthesize-gemini-audio.ts), and whether each option keeps pace consistent across the audio segments joined into one narration track. Repository observations are from commit `65043649`. No paid API calls were made for this note; the pace measurements below come from the archived [Gemini 3.8 comparison](gemini-3.8-tts-comparison.md).

**Short answer:** the model has no speaking-rate parameter. The only native pace control is natural-language direction in the `speech_metadata.style` annotation, which Google documents with the examples "speaking rapidly" and "speaking slowly". It is free text, so it can share one string with the narration-language style that policy 5 already sends. Google gives no reliability figures, and warns that extra style text increases voice drift. The alternative is to time-stretch the 24 kHz PCM before MP3 encoding with a pitch-preserving algorithm. Sonic (Apache-2.0, plain C, built for speech, used by Android's Media3 player for playback speed) is the best fit, compiled to WebAssembly the way Cup already builds LAME. Its CPU cost is a small fraction of the Worker's 5-minute limit. A time-stretch applies exactly the same factor to every segment, whereas a style direction adds a model-dependent change on top of a baseline that already varies by about ±12% between segments. The prototype should compare both by ear: style variants first because they cost no code, and Sonic at a few factors, rendered offline.

## How Cup calls the model today

`synthesizeGeminiAudio` sends, per audio segment, one `user_input` text block whose `text` is the narration text verbatim. Under policy 5, when the narration language is speakable, the block carries one annotation `{ type: "speech_metadata", style: "Read in <Language> with standard <Language> pronunciation." }` and `generation_config.speech_config[0]` carries `voice: "Charon"` and `language: <tag>`. For any other language, both the annotation and `language` are left out. `response_format` asks for `audio/l16` at 24 kHz, and [`produceAudioSegment`](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/65043649/libs/audiobook-production/src/produce-audio-segment.ts) encodes the returned PCM with the LAME WebAssembly module in [`@cup/mp3-encoding`](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/blob/65043649/libs/mp3-encoding/scripts/build-encoder.sh) and measures the stored duration with `analyzeMp3`. A segment holds at most 2,000 characters (`MAX_CHUNK_CHARACTERS` in `narration-chunking.ts`), which at the 80 ms per character that `estimateAudioDuration` assumes is about 160 s of audio.

## Native controls

### No speaking-rate parameter

None of the documented request fields set a speaking rate. The Interactions API's speech configuration has only `voice`, `language` and `speaker`; `generation_config` has `seed`, `stop_sequences`, `max_output_tokens`, thinking, tool and transcription settings, but no `temperature`, rate or speed; and `AudioResponseFormat` has `mime_type`, `delivery`, `sample_rate` and `bit_rate`, which describe encoding, not delivery ([create interaction](https://aistudio.google.com/api-reference/interactions-api/create-interaction.md)). The model page and the TTS guide do not mention one either ([model page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts), [TTS guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation)).

### Natural-language direction in `speech_metadata.style`

This is the documented pace control. The prompting guide's [pacing and pauses](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#pacing-and-pauses) names three levels: punctuation, the inline tags `<short pause>` and `<long pause>`, and turn-level pace through `"style": "speaking rapidly"` or `"style": "speaking slowly"`. The [style vs. inline tags](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#style-vs-inline-tags) table lists overall pace as a turn-level attribute that belongs in `style`, and the migration guide moves sustained directions such as "speaking slowly" out of the transcript and into `style` ([model page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts)). The reference describes `style` only as "Style instruction for the speech synthesis" ([create interaction](https://aistudio.google.com/api-reference/interactions-api/create-interaction.md)).

What the sources do not say:

- **How much faster.** Google's examples are "rapidly" and "slowly", not "a little faster". No source documents a graded or numeric direction, such as "about 10% faster", or whether the model follows one.
- **How reliably.** Google gives no measurement of style adherence. Its guidance is qualitative: start with an empty `style`, add a short string only when needed, and reuse "that exact short string across turns when you want a consistent baseline" ([recommended workflow](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#recommended-workflow)). It also says that "extra prompt text increases drift" and that the 3.8 models anchor on the voice reference first ([consistency and what to avoid](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#consistency-and-what-to-avoid)). A pace direction is therefore a request the model may follow to a varying degree, not a setting.

Cup's own probes give some indirect evidence. In the [language probes](gemini-3.8-tts-language.md#probes), a style asking for German pronunciation moved a names-only byline toward German in one of two samples, and adding a style left the audio length unchanged, so the model does not speak the style text. The [listening prototype](narration-language-by-ear.md) then chose a constant language style for every segment. No probe has tested a pace direction.

### Combining pace with the narration-language style

`style` is a single free-text string per annotation, so a pace direction can be appended to the language sentence, for example "Read in German with standard German pronunciation, speaking a little faster." No source documents several `speech_metadata` annotations on one text block. The reference gives `SpeechAnnotation` `start_index` and `end_index` byte offsets, which suggest annotations can cover parts of a block, but it does not say how overlapping annotations combine, so one combined string is the safe shape.

Two consequences for Cup:

- Policy 5 sends no annotation for a language that is not speakable. A pace direction would need an annotation on every segment, so the next policy has to send a pace-only style when there is no language style.
- A longer style is more of the prompt text that Google says increases drift. Whether it also weakens the language effect on names-only segments, the case policy 5 fixed ([#193](https://github.com/patricktree/cup/issues/193)), can only be checked by listening. A names-only segment is also only a few words long, so its pace is hard to hear and hard to measure.

### Other native routes

- **Inline tags and punctuation** only add or remove pauses at exact points ([pacing and pauses](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#pacing-and-pauses)). Cup sends source text verbatim, so editing it is not an option, and on 3.8 text directions "may be spoken aloud" ([model page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts)).
- **`system_instruction`** is rejected for this model ("Developer instruction is not enabled for this model", [language probes](gemini-3.8-tts-language.md#can-instructions-be-sent-separately-from-the-spoken-text)).
- **Voice design** creates a stored custom voice from a description of "age, vocal timbre, accent, and baseline delivery", and Flash-Lite supports it ([Voice design](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/voice-design)). A brisker baseline delivery might be designed in, but the page does not mention pace, the voice would no longer be Charon, and stored voices expire after one year. That is a larger change than the map asks for, so it is not recommended here.

## Post-processing: time-stretching the PCM

A time-stretch shortens audio by a constant factor while keeping its pitch. It would run in `produceAudioSegment` between synthesis and `encodePcmAsMp3`, on the 16-bit mono 24 kHz PCM the model returns. Plain resampling, which is what a faster playback rate does, is not an option: a factor of 1.1 raises pitch by 12 × log2(1.1) ≈ 1.65 semitones, which audibly changes the voice.

Sonic's author claims "Sonic is better for speech, while WSOLA is better for music", and that PICOLA is the best available algorithm for speech below 2× ([docs](https://github.com/waywardgeek/sonic/blob/master/doc/index.md)). That is the author's own claim, but an independent vendor relies on the same code for speech: Android's Media3 player implements playback speed with a processor that "uses the Sonic library to modify audio speed/pitch/sample rate" ([`SonicAudioProcessor`](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/media3/common/audio/SonicAudioProcessor)) for any speed an app sets, so Sonic carries speech playback speed in apps built on Media3. None of the sources publishes listening results for 5–20% speedups. The step is small, though: all three libraries claim their best quality for modest factors, and audible artefacts in time-domain methods (doubled or dropped pitch periods, "phasiness") grow with the factor. The prototype has to judge 1.05 to 1.2 by ear.

### CPU, memory and bundle size against Worker limits

Synthesis runs in a Workflow step of the `cloudflare-worker` app, which sets `limits.cpu_ms` to 300,000 (`apps/cloudflare-worker/wrangler.jsonc`). Workflows applies that limit to each step ("Individual steps are subject to the configured CPU time limit", [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/)), and 5 minutes is the maximum on the Paid plan ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)).

Sonic's published benchmark sped up 9 h 28 min of 11 kHz mono audio in 50.8 s on a 2011 laptop ([repository](https://github.com/waywardgeek/sonic)), about 670 times faster than real time. Scaled to 24 kHz (2.2 times as many samples), that is still about 300 times real time, so the longest segment of about 160 s takes on the order of half a second of CPU, even before allowing for faster current hardware. Allowing a generous factor of two or three for WebAssembly, it stays at a second or two, which is less than 1% of the step's CPU limit and in the same range as the MP3 encoding Cup already does there. This is an estimate from the published benchmark, not a measurement in a Worker. SoundTouch's README similarly reports real-time CD-quality processing on a Pentium 133 in quick mode ([README](https://www.surina.net/soundtouch/README.html)). Signalsmith Stretch, working in the frequency domain, will cost more and would need measuring.

A 160 s segment is 7.7 MB of 16-bit PCM, and a stretched copy is a little smaller, well inside the 128 MB isolate memory limit, which counts WebAssembly allocations ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). A Sonic module should be tens of kilobytes, against the 130 KB LAME module and a 64 MiB bundle limit.

Integration follows Cup's existing pattern: a pinned, reproducible Emscripten build like `libs/mp3-encoding/scripts/build-encoder.sh`, plus an entry in a third-party notice (Apache-2.0 asks for the license and notice to be kept). Sonic's stream API needs `sonicFlushStream` at the end of each segment so the tail is not lost.

## Pace consistency across joined audio segments

Each audio segment is a separate request, and the [join](join-segment-mp3s.md) concatenates their MP3s without crossfading, so any pace difference between neighbouring segments is heard at the boundary.

**Today's baseline already varies.** In the archived Flash-Lite run (voice Charon, no style, [comparison](gemini-3.8-tts-comparison.md)), the twelve chunks had these durations per character:

The two short headings include leading and trailing silence, so they overstate it. The ten chunks over 150 characters still range from 57 to 74 ms per character, about ±12% around the middle. Characters per second also depend on the text (numbers, abbreviations, punctuation), so this is not all pace variation, but it shows the spread a speedup of 5–20% sits on top of. It also shows that the 80 ms per character in `estimateAudioDuration` is a conservative estimate for this model.

**Style direction.** Each segment is a fresh generation, so the effect of "a little faster" can differ from segment to segment, on top of the baseline spread. Google's advice to reuse one exact short string is the only documented mitigation ([recommended workflow](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation#recommended-workflow)). Short and names-only segments have the least context and are the likeliest to differ. Whether a direction narrows or widens the spread is unknown until measured.

**Time-stretch.** The same factor applies to every segment, so pace relative to today is exactly consistent: the spread between segments stays as it is, scaled by the factor, and the voice and accent stay as policy 5 produces them. The gaps at segment boundaries (the model's leading and trailing silence, and the 50–60 ms the join adds) shrink with the speech only where they are inside the stretched PCM; the join's encoder padding is added after stretching and stays the same. Stored durations stay exact, because `analyzeMp3` measures the encoded audio after stretching.

**Testing.** A time-stretch is deterministic, so a unit test can check that output length is input length divided by the factor, within a frame. A style direction is not, so guarding it needs either the paid narration evals or a one-off listening and duration check over a sample of segments.

## Recommendation for the pace prototype

1. **Measure style directions first, because they cost no code.** Synthesize a fixed sample of segments, in English and German and including a names-only byline, with today's policy 5 request and with two or three pace phrasings: one combined with the language style ("Read in German with standard German pronunciation, speaking a little faster."), Google's "speaking rapidly", and a pace-only style for a non-speakable language. Use two or three samples per condition, because generation is not deterministic. Compare ms per character against the baseline and listen for whether names-only segments keep their German pronunciation and whether pace jumps between neighbouring segments.
2. **Render Sonic time-stretches offline at 1.05, 1.10, 1.15 and 1.20** from the baseline PCM of the same sample, with the Sonic command-line tool or a Node script around a WebAssembly build. FFmpeg's `atempo` (WSOLA) at the same factors is a cheap second reference that shows whether Sonic's speech-specific method is audibly better. This needs no further paid calls beyond the baseline.
3. **Pick by ear on one listening page**, as the [narration-language prototype](narration-language-by-ear.md) did. If a style direction gives a clear and consistent speedup without hurting the language, it is the simplest change: a new policy version with a different style string. Otherwise, a Sonic stretch at the chosen factor gives a pace that is exactly the same across segments, at the cost of a second WebAssembly module and a small amount of CPU per segment.
4. **Do not pursue** a numeric rate parameter (none exists), inline tags or text directions (they only add pauses or are spoken), `system_instruction` (rejected), plain resampling (shifts pitch), or Voice design (changes the voice).

Whichever wins, the measured ms per character at the chosen pace is what the map's duration-reservation question needs.
